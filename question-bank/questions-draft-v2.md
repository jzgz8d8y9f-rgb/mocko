# Mocko Question Bank — Draft v2

Same structure and sourcing rules as v1 (see that file's header note on the copyright situation with the
uploaded guide — nothing here is copied from it, everything is original phrasing). This pass roughly
triples the bank: every question from v1 got two freshly-reworded siblings that test the same underlying
skill or concept from a different angle, scenario, or level of specificity — not just a synonym swap.

Organized by Type → Subsection → (Behavioral / Technical, where applicable) → Difficulty.

## General

### HireVue

#### Behavioral

**Easy**
- Tell me about yourself.
- Walk me through your background in a couple of minutes.
- Give me a quick summary of who you are, professionally.
- What are your long-term career goals?
- Where do you want your career to be headed five or ten years from now?
- What kind of work do you want to be doing later in your career?
- Why are you interested in this company?
- What drew you to apply here specifically?
- What do you know about us, and why does it appeal to you?
- What would you say is your biggest strength?
- What's the skill you'd point to first if someone asked what you're best at?
- What do people usually compliment you on at work or school?
- What's one thing you're working on improving about yourself?
- What's an area of your work you're actively trying to get better at?
- If you had to name a weakness, what would it be?
- What do you like to do outside of school or work?
- How do you spend your time when you're not working or studying?
- Tell me about a hobby or interest that isn't on your resume.

**Medium**
- Tell me about a time you had to work with someone whose style was very different from yours.
- Describe a time you partnered with someone who approached problems completely differently than you do.
- Tell me about working with a teammate you didn't naturally click with.
- Describe a moment when you didn't have enough information to complete a task. What did you do?
- Tell me about a time you had to make progress on something despite missing key details.
- Walk me through a situation where you had to fill in gaps with your own judgment.
- Tell me about a time you had to stay calm under a tight deadline.
- Describe a moment when everything felt like it was due at once. How did you get through it?
- Tell me about handling pressure when there wasn't much time to think.
- Walk me through a time you disagreed with a decision your team made.
- Tell me about a call your group made that you didn't personally agree with.
- Describe a time you voiced a different opinion than the rest of your team.
- Describe a situation where you had to learn something new very quickly.
- Tell me about picking up a skill on short notice because you had no choice.
- Walk me through a time you were thrown into something unfamiliar and had to adapt fast.
- What's a piece of feedback that changed how you work?
- Tell me about feedback that actually shifted your approach afterward.
- Describe a comment or critique that stuck with you and changed your habits.

**Hard**
- Tell me about the last time you let a teammate down. What would you do differently now?
- Describe a moment you didn't come through for someone who was counting on you.
- Walk me through a time your absence or mistake put a colleague in a tough spot.
- Describe a time a plan you were responsible for fell apart. How did you handle it in the moment?
- Tell me about something you owned that collapsed partway through. What did you do right then?
- Walk me through a project of yours that went sideways, and your first move when it did.
- Tell me about a time you had to deliver bad news to someone who wasn't expecting it.
- Describe a conversation where you had to tell someone something they really didn't want to hear.
- Walk me through breaking unwelcome news to a group that was counting on a different outcome.
- What's the most significant mistake you've made professionally, and what changed afterward?
- Tell me about your biggest career misstep so far and what it taught you.
- Describe an error early in your career that still shapes how you work today.
- Tell me about a time your judgment was wrong and you found out the hard way.
- Describe a decision that felt right at the time but turned out to be a mistake.
- Walk me through a call you made that you'd take back if you could.

#### Technical

**Easy**
- What are the three core financial statements, and what does each one tell you?
- Name the three financial statements and describe, in one sentence each, what they show.
- Walk me through what the income statement, balance sheet, and cash flow statement each measure.
- What's the difference between Enterprise Value and Equity Value?
- Explain Enterprise Value versus Equity Value the way you'd explain it to a friend outside finance.
- If a company has debt and cash on its balance sheet, how does that affect the gap between its Equity Value and Enterprise Value?
- In plain terms, what does the time value of money mean?
- Why is a dollar today worth more than a dollar a year from now?
- Explain the core idea behind discounting future cash flows.
- What is EBITDA, and why do people use it instead of net income?
- Walk me through what EBITDA strips out of net income and why that's useful for comparing companies.
- Why might two companies with similar revenue have very different net income but similar EBITDA?
- Why would a company be worth more to a strategic acquirer than to a financial buyer?
- What kinds of value can a strategic buyer capture that a private equity firm typically can't?
- Explain why synergies matter more to one type of buyer than another.
- What's the difference between accounts receivable and accounts payable?
- Explain, with a simple example, how AR and AP show up differently on the balance sheet.
- If a company's accounts receivable balance grows, what does that tell you about its cash collection?

**Medium**
- Walk me through how the three financial statements connect to one another.
- If net income increases by $10, trace how that flows through the other two statements.
- Explain the linkages between the income statement, balance sheet, and cash flow statement using a real example.
- A company buys a piece of equipment for cash. How does that flow through all three statements?
- Walk me through the full statement impact of purchasing $50 of inventory on credit.
- A company takes out a loan to fund a new project. Trace that transaction through all three statements.
- What's the difference between unlevered and levered free cash flow, and when would you use each?
- Explain why a DCF built off unlevered free cash flow needs to be discounted differently than one using levered free cash flow.
- Walk me through how you'd convert Net Income into Unlevered Free Cash Flow.
- Explain how you'd build out a basic DCF, step by step.
- Walk me through the core building blocks of a DCF from projecting cash flows to arriving at a value.
- If you only had 60 seconds, how would you summarize what a DCF actually does?
- What is WACC, and intuitively, why does it matter for valuation?
- Explain what the "weighted" part of WACC actually refers to.
- Why does a company's WACC change if its mix of debt and equity changes?
- Walk me through the three most common valuation methodologies, and one advantage and disadvantage of each.
- Compare Comparable Companies, Precedent Transactions, and DCF — where does each tend to over- or under-value a business?
- If you had to pick just one valuation method under time pressure, which would you choose and why?
- What happens to Goodwill on the balance sheet after an acquisition?
- Explain where Goodwill comes from and what it represents economically.
- If an acquirer overpays for a target, how does that show up in the financial statements afterward?
- Why might two companies with the same Enterprise Value have very different Equity Values?
- Explain how differing capital structures can produce identical Enterprise Values but very different Equity Values.
- Two companies generate the same operating cash flow but have very different debt loads. How does that affect their relative Equity Values?

**Hard**
- Walk me through how you'd calculate Terminal Value in a DCF, and defend which method you'd use and why.
- Compare the Gordon Growth method and the Exit Multiple method for Terminal Value, and explain when each is more appropriate.
- A DCF's Terminal Value ends up being 80% of the total valuation. Is that a red flag, and how would you sanity-check it?
- A deal is funded with 100% stock. Under what conditions would that deal be accretive versus dilutive to EPS?
- Walk me through the mechanics of why a stock deal is accretive when the acquirer's P/E is higher than the target's.
- If the target has a much higher growth rate than the acquirer, how might that change whether a stock deal is accretive?
- How would you set up a basic LBO model, and what are the two or three assumptions that move the returns the most?
- Rank the entry multiple, exit multiple, and leverage level by how much they typically drive LBO returns, and explain why.
- If you could only stress-test one assumption in an LBO model before presenting it, which would it be and why?
- Explain how a Deferred Tax Liability gets created in an M&A deal, and why it matters for the model.
- Walk me through why a step-up in asset basis in an acquisition creates a Deferred Tax Liability.
- How does the presence of a DTL affect the Purchase Price Allocation in a merger model?
- Would you rather maximize IRR or the money-on-money multiple on a deal, and why might those two goals conflict?
- Explain a scenario where a shorter holding period boosts IRR but hurts the total multiple returned.
- Why might a private equity fund prioritize IRR over MoM even when MoM is what limited partners ultimately care about?
- Walk me through how a change in working capital flows into a cash flow statement and why it matters for a DCF.
- If a company's working capital needs increase as it grows, how does that affect its Free Cash Flow projections?
- Explain why an increase in Accounts Receivable is treated as a use of cash even though it represents revenue already earned.
- How would you decide between using the Public Comps and the Precedent Transactions method if they gave you very different answers?
- Precedent Transactions typically imply a higher value than Public Comps — explain why, and how you'd reconcile the two in a final valuation range.
- If your DCF, Comps, and Precedent Transactions all suggest meaningfully different values, walk me through how you'd present that range to a client.

**Easy**
- What is a leveraged buyout, in your own words, and why does using debt make sense for the buyer?

**Hard**

- Walk me through decomposing an LBO's IRR into its component drivers when there's no multiple expansion.
- How would you set up the mandatory versus optional debt paydown in an LBO model?
- Explain the difference between a cash sweep and scheduled amortization in an LBO model.
- Walk me through how excess cash flow gets allocated to debt paydown in a typical LBO structure.
- Walk me through how you'd decide between a dividend recap and an early exit for a portfolio company that's performing well.
- Compare the trade-offs of recapitalizing a strong-performing portfolio company versus selling it early.
- If a portfolio company is outperforming, what factors would push a fund toward a dividend recap instead of a sale?
- What's the difference between maximizing IRR and maximizing MoM, and how would a 3-year hold versus a 7-year hold change which one you optimize for?
- Explain a scenario where a fund would knowingly sacrifice IRR to protect its money-on-money multiple.
- Why might a shorter hold period inflate IRR while actually delivering a worse total return to investors?
- How does an add-on acquisition inside an existing portfolio company change the return profile of the original deal?
- Walk me through how bolt-on acquisitions can boost a platform company's exit multiple.
- Explain how buying smaller companies into a larger portfolio platform creates value beyond simple EBITDA addition.
